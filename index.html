READ THE GENERATOR, NOT A STORY.

Association is
not explanation.

All six signals are invented and unitless. They are not asset returns, forecasts, diversification advice or evidence about a market.

One set of observations

Pearson r is the centered cross-product divided by the product of centered lengths. The matrix, scatter and selected rolling point use exactly the same window. Sample covariance and standard deviations use n − 1.

Small r is not independence

Signal E is a noisy square of A, so a visible curved relationship can have a small linear correlation. Inspect A × E. Correlation does not prove cause and effect. A large magnitude does not make a relationship stable.

A flat signal's r is undefined

In the flat model, F is zero at every observation. Every correlation involving F, including its diagonal, is undefined and shown as NA. It is never replaced with zero or one.

The exact synthetic model & numerical choices

Each observation draws six successive xorshift32 values, scaled to approximately uniform [−√3, +√3): f, a, b, c, e, h. A = f; B = 0.8f + 0.6a; C = −0.6f + 0.8b; D = 0.7c + 0.3a; E = f² − 1 + 0.25e; F = h. The flip model changes B's f coefficient to −0.8 starting at observation 121. The flat model keeps the steady coefficients and sets F = 0. The seed repeats exactly in this implementation.

First-window r always uses observations 1 through the selected length, not the previous view. Change in r is current minus first-window r, in unitless correlation points. Samples are finite; empirical correlations need not equal generator coefficients. No significance tests, confidence intervals, missing-data handling or real data are modeled.

Calculation uses shifted, compensated centered sums on bounded generated signals, with r clipped to [−1, +1] for floating-point rounding. Raw statistics below use six decimal places; rounding is not exact arithmetic. Rolling windows are recomputed, not estimated from prior rounded values.
